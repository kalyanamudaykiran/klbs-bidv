Analytics Labs 1.1 – 1.3 · Interactive simulations

Learn by doing: classify, interview, then replay a lunch rush. All data are simulated in your browser to resemble the Guild mess scans (numbers will differ slightly from the real CSV).

The case · The lunch rush at the Guild mess

Every weekday, hundreds of students reach the Guild mess at almost the same time. Between about 12:30 and 13:30 the queues grow, students wait far longer than at the start or end of lunch, and some may give up and leave. The mess committee wants an operator dashboard that helps the staff react during lunch, and a committee view that shows whether things improve week by week.

What we know

  • 13,972 payment scans over 12 lunches (weeks 7–8, 11:30–14:30). Week 7 was exam week.
  • Average wait is about 50 s off-peak and about 120 s in the rush.
  • 8 counters are open today. A standby counter exists but opens by habit, not by data.
  • Target: 95% of students served within 5 minutes (the 95th-percentile wait is about 245 s today).

Who is involved

  • Lakshmi, mess manager: staffs the lunch.
  • Ravi, counter supervisor: watches the queue.
  • Prof. Rao, committee chair: owns the budget and the target.
  • Dean’s office and Finance: ask for volumes and revenue.
  • Registrar: protects student data.

Scans per 15-minute slot, average lunch

Orange bars: the rush window 12:30–13:29. Demand more than triples in an hour.

The problem: nobody can say, in numbers, what happens, why, what is coming, and what to do. Questions come from everywhere and nobody knows which dashboard to build, how fresh it must be, or whether the data even exist.

Lab 1.3 · Replay a lunch: how fresh must the dashboard be?

Thin line = true 10-minute rolling mean wait. Thick line = what the dashboard shows after refreshing every R minutes plus 2 min lag. Predict the delay first, then press Play.

All 12 lunches · mean delay vs the latency budget

Budget = 10·log₂(1.25) = 3.22 min keeps 80% of the value of acting. Red line = budget.

Power BI service: scheduled refresh allows up to 8 per day on Pro and 48 on Premium Per User or Fabric. Neither gets near R = 1–3 min, so a live operator view needs DirectQuery page refresh, streaming or an event push; the committee view is fine on a daily refresh.

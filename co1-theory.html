CO1 / COI1 / LECTURE 1 · BI & DECISIONS

BI turns data into decisions

•Data: raw facts — a scan record, a till receipt

•Information: data with context — waits up 22%

•Insight: the reason — the 12:30 rush beats 8 counters

•Action: the decision — open the standby counter

​

In practice

Walmart stocks ahead of storms · Netflix ranks your first title · Swiggy places riders ahead of demand · banks spot missed EMIs early

Value appears only when a decision changes — visibility, control, learning, foresight

CO1 / COI1 / LECTURE 1 · DASHBOARDS

Four dashboard types

​

Strategic

CEO and board · quarterly · 5–10 KPIs

​

Tactical

Department heads · weekly · drill-down

​

Operational

Supervisors · real time · alerts

​

Analytical

Analysts · on demand · explore

Avoid: one dashboard for everyone · too many KPIs · numbers without targets · real-time data for a monthly decision

CO1 / COI2 / LECTURE 2 · THE DATA LIFECYCLE

Preparing data for BI

•Create, store, prepare, use, share, archive, delete — each step has an owner

•Governance, security and quality run through every step (DPDP Act 2023)

•Connect and model sources: one fact table, clean dimensions, a clear grain

•Profile and clean before BI: missing values, duplicates, invalid types, inconsistent entries

​

One line each

Warehouse = curated · Lake = raw · Semantic layer = shared definitions · Data quality = a job, not a step

CO1 / COI3 / LECTURE 3 · FRAMING

Work backwards from the decision

Decision → business question → metric → data requirement → visual

Start from the decision, not from the data — work backwards

​

A KPI is complete when it has

name · formula · unit · time grain · source · owner · target

​

SMART-D

Specific · Measurable · Actionable · Relevant · Time-bound · Data-available — if a number cannot be obtained, it is not a KPI

CO1 / COI3 / LECTURE 3 · STAKEHOLDERS

People before charts

Who is in the room

Sponsor · business owner · end users · data stewards · IT · finance · compliance

Power–interest grid: manage sponsors and owners closely; keep finance satisfied; keep users informed and consulted

​

MoSCoW, mess rules

M · Must — a lunch-time decision and the data exist

S · Should — a decision after lunch and the data exist

C · Could — no decision named yet

W · Won’t — the data do not exist
